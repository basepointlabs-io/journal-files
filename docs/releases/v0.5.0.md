**Revit Doctor** (called Revit Journal Analyzer up to 0.4.0) watches the computer while Revit runs and explains Revit journal files in plain English. It works entirely on your computer: no AI, no uploads.

## What's new in 0.5.0

### A new name and a new look
- The program is now **Revit Doctor**, with a new logo. The installer upgrades an installed Revit Journal Analyzer in place and carries your settings over.
- **"Right now" is the start screen.** Free memory is the main figure, with a chart of the last half hour and the zone where memory counts as low. The memory and disk figures turn orange and then red as they run out, and the state at the top has its own sign: a check, a warning or an alarm.
- One compact row for each running Revit, and a coloured line only when something needs attention. The window is narrower and long report text wraps instead of being cut off.
- The processor meter and the "What's wrong with Revit?" picker are gone. Notifications still open the answer for what happened.

### The model against the computer's memory
- For each running Revit the app shows the **size of the open model, its linked models and the number of open views**, and compares the model with Autodesk's memory guidance (16 GB of RAM for a model of about 300 MB, 32 GB for about 600 MB, 64 GB for about 1 GB).
- **Several Revit at once:** a summary of everything running, a warning when the open models together are too large for the computer, and the version picker follows the Revit versions that are running.
- The same comparison appears in the report of a finished session, next to what Revit actually used.

### Disk space
- The app **records free disk space** every few minutes, so a report can say how full the disk was when a session ended, even when you open the report after a restart.
- Below 5 GB the full disk becomes the headline of the report; below Autodesk's minimum of 30 GB it is reported.

### Revit that closes or does not start
- New findings for a Revit that **stops while starting**, **closes by itself right after starting**, or **does not start again** after a session that ended without a shutdown record. The report names the stage, the last add-in that started and the add-ins that were active in the last seconds.
- A finding for the "Too many tabs have been created by the API" dialog (more than 20 add-ins with their own ribbon tab).

### Advice names its source
- Every step in the new findings ends with the **Autodesk article that recommends it**, and the articles are listed as links under the finding.

### Quieter warnings
- The memory forecast no longer warns while there is plenty of memory: it appears only when less than a quarter is free, and notifies when about ten minutes are left.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11, x64 or ARM64. Nothing else to install (.NET is included).
- Reads Revit's journals in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Autodesk\Revit\Autodesk Revit 20XX\Journals`, the Windows Application event log (for crash proof), the computer's resource counters and the size of model files on this computer. Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Known limitations

- The new overview, the notifications and the upgrade from Revit Journal Analyzer are covered by automated tests and rendered screens; please report how they behave during real work.
- Sizes of linked models are known only on the computer the journal comes from, and links stay counted until that Revit is closed.
- Disk space at the end of a session is known only for sessions recorded while the app was running.
- Only the newer findings cite Autodesk articles step by step; the rest of the rule base lists its sources per finding.
